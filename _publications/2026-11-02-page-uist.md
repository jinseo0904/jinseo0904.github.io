abstract: "Collecting in-situ behavioral data to support research studies often involves prompting repeated ecological momentary assessments (EMAs) on smartphones and wearables. Sustained engagement, however, remains a major challenge because study participants often experience survey fatigue and declining motivation over time. To address this challenge, we introduce Personalized Adaptive Games for Engagement (PAGE): a novel LLM-powered approach that integrates data collection with a personalized game experience. PAGE uses a participant’s previously collected data to dynamically generate personalized puzzles, embedding behavioral data collection within engaging gameplay. We deployed a prototype using a variant of a word wheel puzzle game in a two-week feasibility study (N=15); while participants responded to EMAs in both weeks of the study, they also received sensor-based personalized puzzles in one of those weeks. We found that most participants (80%) preferred the week with personalized puzzles. Participants also contributed richer EMA data (72 additional unique contextual tags) and 1,188 additional uncompensated responses via a feasible in-game data collection channel during the puzzle week. We discuss design guide lines for developing context-aware personalized games to support engagement, potentially for use in longitudinal studies."
